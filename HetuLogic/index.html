Mapping, Logical Fallacies & Knowledge Graphs

Inspect how Nyaya rules map to AI systems, test logical fallacies, and explore Vaisheshika ontology as an interactive Knowledge Graph.

Concept 1 • Rules

Vyapti ⟷ Expert System Rules

In Nyaya: Vyapti means an unbreakable rule—whenever X occurs, Y must follow (e.g., wherever there is smoke, there is fire).

In Modern AI: Rule-based expert systems encode this exact pattern as IF Condition THEN Conclusion logic rules.

Test Rule Direction (Vyapti Check):
VALID RULE Smoke reliably indicates fire. Clear lakes (negative cases) never produce smoke.
Concept 2 • Evidence

Hetu ⟷ AI Feature Triggers

In Nyaya: The Hetu is the observed sign or clue (like smoke) that must pass 5 strict checks to be trustworthy.

In Modern AI: Input features and sensor thresholds (e.g., CPU temp > 95°C) act as the evidence triggers that activate decisions.

Click a Check to Inspect Trustworthy Evidence:
Pakshadharmata The clue must genuinely exist in the specific case being tested, preventing ungrounded guesses.
Concept 3 • Output

Nigamana ⟷ Explainable AI Output

In Nyaya: Nigamana is the final confirmed conclusion—valid only after every step and real-world example has been verified.

In Modern AI: Mirrors Explainable AI (XAI), where an AI outputs a final decision along with a transparent, auditable reasoning trail.

Nyaya (5 Steps) vs. Aristotle (3 Steps)

Unlike Aristotle's purely abstract 3-step logic, Nyaya requires a real-world verified example (Udaharana) before concluding.

Aristotle (3 steps): Major Premise → Minor Premise → Conclusion
Nyaya (5 steps): Claim → Clue → Rule + Example → Application → Conclusion
Fallacy 1

Savyabhichara (Inconsistent Clue)

A clue that appears in both positive and negative cases, so it cannot prove the claim.

AI Equivalent: Spurious correlations & model hallucinations.
Fallacy 2

Viruddha (Contradictory Clue)

A reason that actually proves the exact opposite of what you claimed.

AI Equivalent: Data poisoning & adversarial sticker attacks.
Fallacy 3

Satpratipaksha (Tied Evidence)

Two equally strong clues push toward opposite conclusions, creating a deadlock.

AI Equivalent: Conflicting guardrails causing agent paralysis.
Fallacy 4

Asiddha (Unproven Premise)

A reason built on an assumption or object that does not actually exist.

AI Equivalent: Corrupted input data or Out-of-Distribution (OOD) inputs.
Fallacy 5

Badhita (Overruled by Direct Fact)

An inferred conclusion that directly contradicts what you can plainly observe.

AI Equivalent: ML prediction overruled by ground-truth safety radar.
